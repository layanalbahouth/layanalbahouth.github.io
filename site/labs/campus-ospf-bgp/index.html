Campus network lab: VLANs, OSPF and BGP

I built this lab in Cisco Packet Tracer during my Tamheer training. It models a small company: three buildings on one campus, routed internally with OSPF, connected through an ISP to a branch office with BGP. When I reopened it later, one PC could no longer reach the branch. Finding out why turned out to be the most useful part.

The topology

The campus follows a three-tier design. Each building has an access switch for the PCs and a distribution switch that routes for that building. The three distribution switches connect to one core switch, and the core connects to the edge router. The links between them are small /30 networks: 10.0.0.0/30, 10.0.0.4/30 and 10.0.0.8/30 inside the campus, and 10.1.0.0/30 onwards towards the edge, ISP and branch.

How the pieces fit

  • Access to distribution: each access switch connects to its distribution switch with two cables bundled into one EtherChannel, configured as an 802.1Q trunk that carries that building’s two VLANs.
  • Gateways: each VLAN’s gateway is a VLAN interface (SVI) on the distribution switch, for example interface Vlan10 with 192.168.10.1.
  • Inside the campus: the distribution switches, the core and the edge router run OSPF in area 0 over small /30 links.
  • Outside the campus: the edge router (AS 65001), the ISP router (AS 65002) and the branch router (AS 65003) exchange routes with BGP. On the core, the branch network appears as an external OSPF route (O E2) learned from the edge router.

The problem: PC 20 couldn’t reach the branch

A ping from PC 20 to PC 70 at the branch failed, while PC 40 could reach PC 70 without trouble. So the BGP side was working, and the problem was somewhere inside the campus.

The core’s routing table showed what was going on:

Core# show ip route
O    192.168.30.0/24 [110/2] via 10.0.0.5
O    192.168.40.0/24 [110/2] via 10.0.0.5
O E2 192.168.70.0/24 [110/20] via 10.1.0.2

Only Building 2’s networks were there. Buildings 1 and 3 were missing completely, even though the core had full OSPF neighbours with all three distribution switches. The neighbours were up, but two of them were advertising nothing for their VLANs.

The cause

On Distribution Switch B1, both VLAN interfaces were up/down, and the log had a clear hint:

%SPANTREE-2-BLOCK_PVID_LOCAL: Blocking Port-channel1 on VLAN0001. Inconsistent port type.

The EtherChannel was a trunk on the access switch but still an access port on the distribution switch. Cisco calls this a type inconsistency: a trunk’s spanning-tree messages arrive on a port that isn’t a trunk, so the switch blocks that port to prevent a loop.

That block had a knock-on effect. A VLAN interface only comes up when at least one switch port in that VLAN is up and forwarding. With the only link to the building blocked, Vlan10 and Vlan20 stayed down, and OSPF had nothing to advertise for them. Distribution Switch B3 had exactly the same mismatch on its port-channel.

B1 had a second, smaller mistake: the gateway for VLAN 20 was set to 192.168.11.1, while PC 20 expected 192.168.20.1.

The fix

! Distribution Switch B1 (the same trunk fix on B3, for Port-channel3)
interface Port-channel1
 switchport trunk encapsulation dot1q
 switchport mode trunk
!
interface Vlan20
 ip address 192.168.20.1 255.255.255.0

As soon as the trunk matched on both ends, the switch logged Port consistency restored, the VLAN interfaces came up, and the missing networks appeared on the core.

Checking it works

PC20> ping 192.168.70.70
Reply from 192.168.70.70: bytes=32 time<1ms TTL=123
Packets: Sent = 4, Received = 4, Lost = 0 (0% loss)

PC 20 now reaches the branch, PC 10 in its own building, and PC 60 in Building 3.

What I took from it

  • OSPF neighbours being up doesn’t mean every network is being advertised. The routing table on the core is the quickest way to see what’s actually missing.
  • A trunk has two ends. When a link misbehaves, check both sides, not only the one you configured.
